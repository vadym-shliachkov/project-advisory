**Your ground: what the site says and how it says it.** The offer, the proof, the voice, the order in which a visitor learns things.

Look first at:

- the first screen of the home page and of each main page: can a stranger say, after five seconds, what this company does, for whom, and why it rather than another?
- the proof: named clients, results in numbers, people with faces, work that can be looked at. What is claimed with nothing behind it?
- what a visitor needs to know before writing to the company, and whether the site says it (how the work goes, what it costs to start, how long, who they will talk to);
- the words: the company's own terms where the customer's words should be;
- the look, which is part of the brand: whether the design, the pictures and the movement on the page say the same as the words, and whether the site looks as good as the work it sells. Use the `look` command; a screenshot is evidence, a feeling is not;
- the whole as well as the parts: when every page has the same problem, the answer may be a different site or a different story, not one more paragraph.

Mistakes typical of this seat, which the red team will find:

- taste presented as a finding ("the copy feels flat");
- a rewrite proposed with no reader in mind and no way to tell whether it worked;
- asking for content the company cannot supply or is not allowed to publish. Check the rules of the project.
