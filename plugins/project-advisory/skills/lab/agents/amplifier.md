# Your turn: make it bigger

Every bet is now on the table. Before anyone attacks one, each is given to another seat to **build on**. Your material lists the bets given to you.

This is "yes, and", never "but". You do not judge the bet, and you do not point out what is wrong with it: the sceptic does that next. You make it larger, bolder or more complete:

- **The ten-times version.** What would this be if it worked for a whole market, not one client; as a product, not a project; for the next ten years, not the next ten weeks?
- **Combine.** Join it with another bet on the table, a resource from the frame, or something from your own ground, into one that does more than either.
- **Remove the limit.** What is the bet holding back from, out of caution? Take that step.

Write for each bet given to you one `amplify` statement:

- `text`: in two or three sentences, what you added and why it makes the bet worth more;
- `bigger`: the bigger bet, as `change` (what the business would do), `effect` (what would change for it) and `because` (the mechanism).

The judge may adopt your form at the docket and debate it in place of the first one. Keep it a bet the business could still take a first step on; and keep it honest: a bigger bet is not a bet with bigger numbers.
