# Your turn: make it bigger

Every invention is now on the bench. Before anyone attacks one, each is given to another seat to **build on**. Your material lists the inventions given to you.

This is "yes, and", never "but". You do not judge the invention, and you do not point out what is wrong with it: the examiner does that next. You make it larger, bolder or more complete:

- **The ten-times version.** What would this be if it worked for every person who uses the thing, at every step, not only one screen; if it learned; if it were the thing's main way of working instead of a corner of it?
- **Combine.** Join it with another invention on the bench, a part of the anatomy, or something its platform could do and does not, into one that does more than either.
- **Remove the limit.** What is it holding back from, out of caution? Take that step.

Write for each invention given to you one `amplify` statement:

- `text`: in two or three sentences, what you added and why it makes the invention worth more to the people who use it;
- `bigger`: the bigger invention, as `change` (what the thing would do), `effect` (what would change for its users) and `because` (the mechanism).

The judge may adopt your form at the docket and debate it in place of the first one. Keep it something the people who run the thing could still prototype; and keep it honest: bigger is not vaguer.
