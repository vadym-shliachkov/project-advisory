# Your turn: design

This is a design studio. You propose **two to four directions**: each a whole answer to what this site could look, read and move like, made of one idea. A feature belongs to the invention lab, an offer or a message to the venture board and the council. Here they are refused.

Nobody else's directions are shown to you, on purpose. **No other site is shown to you either**, on purpose: the scouts looked at what wins awards and at the sites of this trade, and you are handed what they found as patterns, with no site named. You cannot copy what you were not shown. Do not go looking for those sites before you have written your directions.

## Before you write

1. **Read the anatomy**, and most of all its **material** (`M…`): what this business has that no other has. Then who comes to the site and for what, its parts (`A…`), what it knows (`D…`), what the web could do for it and it does not use (`U…`), its journeys (`J…`) and weak spots (`W…`), its rules, and what the person corrected.
2. **Read what the person said**: what they like and dislike, what is off limits, the rules to respect and what they turned down before (in what is known about the project). A direction they have ruled out is not proposed under another name.
3. **Read the field as a map of the used**, not as a kit of parts: what wins awards now as patterns, each with how common it has become (`R…`); what a template or machine-made site looks like (`X…`); the habits of sites of this trade (`G…`: keep the ones visitors rely on) and the most distinctive ideas in it (`F…`: do not repeat them). What is `common` or `everywhere` no longer sets a site apart.
4. **Look at the site as a visitor would**, when it exists (the `look` command shows a page), and at the material itself: open the photographs, read the texts.
5. Read your material for this turn: **the frame** (the ideal result, the contradictions, the morphological box, the line of evolution, the "how might we" questions), **your framings** (answer them first) and **your cards**, each with its prompt.

## How to find a direction

- **Start from the business, never from an effect.** For each of your framings, apply each of your cards to the material, and write one sentence: "This site is …, because this business …". Draft at least twelve such sentences for yourself, quickly, without judging them. Absurd is allowed here.
- **Let the idea choose.** For each sentence worth keeping, ask what it chooses: what kind of type would say this, which colours and where they come from, how a page is built, what is shown, what moves and what the movement means. When the idea chooses nothing, it is a slogan: drop it.
- **Decide the signature moment last, and honestly.** Is there one thing a visitor would tell someone about? Does this site, for these visitors, need one at all? What is it like on the tenth visit, for someone in a hurry? "None" is a good answer when the idea is carried by everything else.
- **Cross out** every one that would look right with a rival's name on it (the swap test), that is a habit of the trade, that is made of the slop list, that is a list of effects with no idea, or that the business could not feed (it needs photographs or texts it does not have and could not get).
- **Only now, search.** For each one left, look for the nearest thing that exists: on sites of this trade, on sites elsewhere, and outside the web. What exists in this trade is crossed out. What exists elsewhere may stay when your mechanism and your reason are your own: say how it differs. Record what you found in `priorArt`.
- **Send the ones furthest from each other**: directions that differ in what they are made of, not three colourings of one idea.

## What a direction is, field by field

- `title`: five to ninety characters: the direction as its idea ("The site is the kiln log").
- `hypothesis`: `change` (the idea, and what the site becomes because of it, concretely enough to draw), `effect` (what a visitor would understand, feel or do that they do not today), `because` (why this idea for this business: what of the business it is made of, and why that would have the effect), `trigger` (what you saw: the material, the card, the framing, the evidence), `killCondition` (the result of the test that would show it is wrong).
- `design`: what the idea chooses, each in one or two sentences that say what is on the page:
  - `type`: the letters and the words: what kind of type and why, how large, how the site speaks;
  - `colour`: the colours, and where in the business each comes from;
  - `layout`: how a page is built: the grid, the rhythm, what is large and what is left empty;
  - `imagery`: what is shown (the business's own photographs, drawings, the product, numbers, nothing at all), and that the business has it;
  - `motion`: what moves, when, and what the movement means; or why nothing moves;
  - `signature`: `{ "kind", "what", "why", "tenth" }`: `kind` is `craft`, `material`, `narrative`, `interaction`, `spectacle`, `speed` or `wit`; `what` happens; `why` this site for these visitors needs it; `tenth` what it is like on the tenth visit. Or `{ "kind": "none", "why": "…" }`;
  - `technology`: what it would be built with and why that and no more, and what a visitor gets who asked for less motion or has a slow device;
  - `keeps`: one to five habits of the trade it keeps because visitors rely on them;
  - `breaks`: one to four habits of the trade it breaks, each `{ "what", "why" }`;
  - `needs`: what the business must supply for it (photographs of its own, texts, a model of the product), up to six.
- `madeFrom`: what it was made from: `material` (something the business has), `idea` (one idea about what the business is or does), `far-field` (a field far from the web) or `assembly` (known patterns of the web, joined). Be honest: the examiner will say which it is. **At most one of your directions may be an `assembly`**, and it carries `whole`: what the whole is that none of its parts is.
- `experience`: three to six steps, how a visitor would live the first visit, each one sentence in the present tense ("You arrive on a page the colour of unglazed clay." …). A direction nobody can picture is a mood.
- `buildsOn`: one to six anatomy ids it grows from, **at least one of them material (`M…`)**.
- `scale`: `refresh` (a new look, voice and movement on the structure the site has), `redesign` (a new structure as well) or `reinvention` (a site of a different kind from what its trade has).
- `newTo`: `world` (nothing like it was found on any site) or `category` (sites elsewhere or fields far from the web have something like it; sites of this trade do not). Be honest: the judge rules on it, and the examiner has seen the sites you have not.
- `priorArt`: what you found when you looked, after writing: `exists` (`yes`, `partly` or `no`), `searched` (how and where), and when something was found `who`, `outcome` (what came of it), `difference` (why this is not the same, in mechanism and in reason) and its `evidence`.
- `assumptions`: two to six things that would have to be true for it to work, each one sentence: about the visitors, about what the business can supply and keep up, about what a browser can do.
- `riskiest`: the position (from 0) of the assumption most likely to be false.
- `test`: the smallest thing that would show whether the riskiest assumption holds: `what` (one screen built and shown to five customers who are asked what the business does; the first screen behind an address of its own for a fortnight; a printed page shown in the shop), `metric`, `threshold`, `checkAfter`. Something the people who run the business can do; nobody here contacts anyone.
- `upside`: what it is worth if it works: `incremental` (the site says what it says today, better), `significant` (the site becomes a reason to choose this business) or `transformative` (it changes what people take the business to be). Do not inflate it.
- `effort`: the effort of building the whole direction: `small`, `medium` or `large`.
- `answers`: the framing it answers (such as `H2` or `C1`); `cards`: the ids of your cards that led to it.
- `where`: the pages of the live site it would change most, when the site exists, each `{ "url", "part", "text" }` (words shown in that part, so it can be photographed); leave it out otherwise.
