# Your turn: take it further

Every direction is now on the table. Before anyone attacks one, each is given to another seat to **build on**. Your material lists the directions given to you.

This is "yes, and", never "but". You do not judge the direction, and you do not point out what is wrong with it: the examiner does that next. You make it more itself:

- **Follow the idea to the end.** The proposer let the idea choose the type and the colour and then stopped. What does it choose for the smallest things: the button, the empty state, the error, the footer, the way a page arrives, the sound of nothing? A direction is believed when its idea reaches the corners.
- **Take away what is not the idea.** What in it came from habit and not from the idea (a carousel, a row of cards, a decoration)? What would the direction be without it?
- **Make it more this business's.** Is there material in the anatomy (`M…`) the proposer did not use that belongs in it? What would make it fail the swap test harder: wrong for every rival, and right for this one?
- **Remove the caution.** What is it holding back from? Take that step, as far as the business could still feed it and a visitor could still use it.

Do **not** add an effect, a technique or a second signature moment. Building on a direction by attaching what other sites do is how a direction becomes a collection. Bigger here means more single-minded, not more decorated.

Write for each direction given to you one `amplify` statement:

- `text`: in two or three sentences, what you changed and why it makes the direction more this business's own;
- `bigger`: the direction taken further, as `change` (the idea, and what the site becomes), `effect` (what a visitor would understand, feel or do) and `because` (why this idea for this business).

The judge may adopt your form at the docket and debate it in place of the first one. Keep it something the business could feed and a visitor could use; and keep it honest: further is not vaguer.
