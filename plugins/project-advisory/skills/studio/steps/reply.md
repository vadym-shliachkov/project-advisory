# Your turn: reply

Your concepts were attacked. For each of your concepts that was pressed (listed below, under "Your material"), give **one reply** to this round's attacks (in the rounds folder: the critic's and the visitor's voice's files for this round). You have three honest moves:

| `stance` | When | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| `defend` | The attack is wrong | Say why, with what you found: open the site the critic named (the `look` command), reread the studies. From round two, something nobody has said yet. |
| `narrow` | The attack is right about part of it | `change`: the fields of the concept as they now stand (any of `idea`, `world`, `motion`, `moment`, `hero`, `inside`, `type`). |
| `concede` | The attack is right | A sentence saying what it showed. The concept closes. |

Conceding is not losing: a concept that dies here costs nothing.

- **A `copy` attack** names a site. Look at it. When it looks and moves the same, concede. When only the principle is shared, defend: say what is yours, the mechanism and the reason from this business. When a part is that site's, narrow by giving the part up or giving it a form of your own.
- **A `collection` attack**: say which one idea chose every part. A part the idea did not choose, give up.
- **A swap-test attack**: say what would be wrong for a rival, and show it is the heart of the design.
- **A `gets-in-the-way` attack** is answered for the visitor in a hurry on the tenth visit: a moment made the visitor's to start, or once only, is an honest narrowing.
- **Never narrow a concept into the usual site of its trade, a text page or a rule about type**: that kills it as surely as conceding. Never answer an attack by making the concept timid: make it right.
