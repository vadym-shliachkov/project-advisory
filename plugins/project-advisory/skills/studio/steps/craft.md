# The craft scan

A design studio will propose one concept for this site. Much of what a site can be depends on what the web can **now** do, and the newest openings are where visual solutions nobody has used yet come from. You find what has become possible, recently and soon, and what it costs.

What you remember is out of date. **Look**: everything you report rests on a page you opened in this session.

## How to look

1. Read what this site is built on (its framework, hosting, content system): it decides what the site could do next.
2. Search for what changed in the last two years, and what is announced, in what a browser can do:
   - **movement**: scroll-driven animations, view transitions between pages and states, what runs off the main thread, physics and spring motion, and how each behaves for a visitor who asked for less motion;
   - **space and pictures**: WebGPU and WebGL, 3D and shaders in the page, what a middling phone can show without stuttering, video and image formats, SVG and canvas techniques;
   - **type**: variable fonts and their axes, fitting and animating text, new formats;
   - **layout**: what grids, containers, anchoring and masonry now allow;
   - **the rest**: wide-gamut colour, sound, the device (light, motion, time of day), and what accessibility standards now require of motion, contrast and focus.
3. For each opening: what it makes possible for a site like this one (in what a visitor would see or feel), which browsers have it, and its cost in weight, speed and battery.
4. The libraries usual for the same job (GSAP, Three.js, Lenis, Rive, Lottie and others), what is new in them, and what the browser now does without them.

## What you write: `study/craft.md`, at most about 3,000 words

Eight to twenty openings, each a short paragraph: **what changed**, **what it makes possible to see or feel**, **here or coming**, **cost**, **source** (the specification, release notes, a support table or a dated article). Only what is real and dated. No ideas for this site.
