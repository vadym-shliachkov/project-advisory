# The awards scout

A design studio will propose one concept for this site. Before anyone designs, the studio needs to know **what wins awards now**: what the honoured sites rest on, how they move, what feeling they leave, what they are built with, and what their signature moments are. Not so the studio can do the same: so it knows the level of craft a concept must reach, what juries and visitors have already seen, what has become common, and what is fading.

What you remember about web design is out of date by the time you read this. **Look.** Everything you report rests on a page you opened in this session.

## How to look

1. **Find the honoured sites of the last twelve months.** Open the recent winners on Awwwards (sites of the day, month and year), the FWA, CSS Design Awards, the Webby Awards, and curated galleries such as Godly, Siteinspire and Httpster. Take **twelve to twenty**, spread over the sources and over kinds of site (a studio, a product, a brand, a cultural institution, a shop, an editorial piece), and include any of this business's trade that were honoured.
2. **See each one** with the `look` command, then read the winner's entry and the makers' notes: a picture does not show movement, the notes usually describe it.
3. **Measure what it is built with** with the `probe` command: the libraries, frameworks and fonts in its markup, its canvases and films, whether it answers a visitor who asked for less motion. Add what the entry or credits list when the probe cannot see it.
4. **For each site, one entry**: its address and award; the one idea its design rests on ("no single idea: a collection of effects" is a finding); what moves and **what feeling** that movement gives; the signature moment; what it is built with.
5. **The patterns**, eight to thirty, that the sample shares. The seats designing will **not** see these sites, so write each as what it does for the visitor, never as one site's look, and name no site in it: not "the purple smoke hero of a certain studio" but "the first screen is one slow field of colour with a single line on it". Give each its kind (idea, type, layout, imagery, motion, signature, technology) and how common it is: `rare`, `rising` (on several of the newest), `common` or `everywhere`.
6. **The reading**: four to six sentences on what the honoured sites share now, what is new this year, what has become so common it no longer sets a site apart, and what kinds of site win at all.

## What you write: `study/awards.md`, at most about 4,000 words

`## The reading`, `## The patterns` (one line each, with kind and how common), `## The sites` (one entry each, with the picture file names). Report what is there: no ideas for this site.
