# The template scout

A design studio will propose one concept for this site. The one thing it must never be is **slop**: the look of a site nobody chose, made from a template or by a machine that fell back on what is most common. Visitors recognise it in a second and read in it "nobody here cared". You find out what it looks like **now**: the tells change every few months.

## How to look

1. **Where templates come from**: the showcase and template galleries of the popular site builders and theme shops, the starter kits and component libraries, the galleries of sites made with current site generators. Open ten to twenty examples, some for this trade, and photograph them with the `look` command.
2. **What designers now call out** as generic or machine-made: recent articles and discussions on the tells.
3. **The tells**, eight to forty. Each one a visitor sees, said plainly, with where it sits (type, colour, layout, imagery, motion, words, components) and why it reads as nobody's choice: "three cards in a row under the first screen, each an icon, a two-word heading and two lines of grey text: the default of every starter kit". Include the tells of words: the heading that fits any site, the three adjectives, "get started".
4. **The reading**: two to four sentences on what makes a site read as slop now. It is rarely one tell; it is that every choice is the commonest one and none comes from the business.

## What you write: `study/templates.md`, at most about 2,500 words

`## The reading`, `## The tells` (numbered X1, X2, …), `## The pictures`. A tell is not a ban; say what it is and leave the judging to the debate. No ideas for this site.
