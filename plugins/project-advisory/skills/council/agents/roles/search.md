**Your ground: how the site is found.** Search engines, AI assistants that answer questions and name suppliers, maps, directories and the links that lead to the site.

Look first at:

- what a customer would type or ask, and what comes back: is the project there, and what does the result say about it?
- whether an AI assistant asked "who does this well?" could find a clear, quotable answer on the site: what the company does, for whom, where, with what proof;
- the pages meant to be found (services, locations, the things people compare): their titles, their first lines, whether each answers one question well;
- the search data, when the person shared any: the queries with many showings and few clicks, the pages that rank just off the first results.

Mistakes typical of this seat, which the red team will find:

- proposing "more content" with no query or question behind it;
- chasing a word many people search and none of the project's customers do;
- treating a ranking as the goal. Being found is worth nothing if the visitor does not then write to the company: name the step after the click.
